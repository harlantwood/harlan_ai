Your work has a reason for existing.

You can feel the pressure to do something with AI. You can also feel when the options in front of you have no soul.

You can feel it pull in both directions — and you don’t have to choose. This is not hesitation. It is discernment. It also happens to be good engineering instinct: the AI systems built without that question are, reliably, the ones that fall apart six months into production.

Build it. Harden it. Automate it.

What I actually build.

Recent client work.

Zero to production

From nothing to real users

Zero to production: products taken from first commit to security-hardened systems with real users depending on them — architecture, build, deployment, and the operational discipline that keeps them standing.

Evaluation

Turning “it seems better” into a number

I design evaluation frameworks and introduce eval-driven development to teams as a core practice — custom simulators running matrix comparisons across models and runs, wired into the tooling developers already use. This is the single highest-leverage thing most AI teams are missing, and the reason so many promising projects stall.

Real-time voice

Conversations without the seam

Voice-to-voice systems built on VAPI, which lets us use any combination of models for transcription, voice synthesis, and the LLM brain — tool calling and custom webhook handling, tuned until the latency stops being something you notice. Getting a model to answer is easy. Getting it to feel like someone listening is the work.

Retrieval

Memory that finds the right thing

A knowledge-graph system that generates nodes and edges from natural-language queries, powering a path-based retrieval architecture that outperforms both conventional RAG and GraphRAG. Twenty-five years of studying semantic graphs, arriving exactly where the field now needs it.

Production

The unglamorous majority

HIPAA-compliant applications, WASM for offline capability, content security policy, CI/CD that developers don’t fight — and that raises quality through AI automation. An AI product is still a software product, and building it like one is what lets it thrive.

The shape I think this is taking.

The monolithic towers of the tech monopolies are giving way to something more like mycelium — lateral, redundant, cooperative, rooted. Not because the towers lost an argument, but because the other shape turns out to carry more life.

What I care about is the difference between tools that amplify a person and tools that harvest one. Almost everything now being built with AI is technically capable of either. Which one it becomes is decided early, in the social and technical architecture, by the people holding the reins. That’s why I do this work.

And I’ll say the quiet part plainly: I think that whatever we build together deserves to be built with heart, integrity, and love infused in every line of code and every corner of the interface. If that sentence makes you feel more at home here rather than less, we will probably get along well.
