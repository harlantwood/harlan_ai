Your work has a reason for existing.

You can feel the pressure to do something with AI. You can also feel when the options in front of you have no soul.

You can feel it pull in both directions — and you don’t have to choose. Most of this industry will tell you that hesitation is a failure of nerve. I think it’s discernment. It also happens to be good engineering instinct: the AI systems built without that question are, reliably, the ones that fall apart six months into production.

You can move quickly and build something you’d be proud to have made. I’ve been there before, and I’ve spent twenty-five years learning how to do both at once.

Build it. Harden it. Automate it.

Three ways I help, whether you’re starting from a blank repo or a system that has begun to drift.

Build

Build it from scratch, ready for the real world.

Your idea or prototype, built to become the actual product — security-hardened, productionized, and architected to scale. Not a demo you have to throw away when it is time to get serious, but the foundation you keep building on.

Harden

Make what you already have better.

I review your existing software and bring it up to production grade: security, hardening, scalability, and the tests and automation that keep it healthy. You are left with systems your own people — or an ordinary developer with AI assistance — can confidently maintain and extend.

Automate

Stand up your AI-native development pipeline.

Set the whole thing up from scratch — the team, the tools, and a task-driven pipeline you can largely forget about, where AI does the heavy lifting and people steer. I love doing this with startups: it is the difference between shipping in weeks and shipping in quarters.

What’s arriving now is real. Most of what’s loud isn’t.

What I actually build.

Recent client work, described plainly. The hard part of each of these is never the part that demos well.

Evaluation

Turning “it seems better” into a number

I design evaluation frameworks and introduce eval-driven development to teams as a core practice — custom simulators running matrix comparisons across models and runs, wired into the tooling developers already use. This is the single highest-leverage thing most AI teams are missing, and the reason so many promising projects stall at eighty percent.

Real-time voice

Conversations without the seam

Voice-to-voice systems built on VAPI and OpenAI’s Realtime API: transcription, tool calling, custom webhook handling, tuned until the latency stops being something you notice. Getting a model to answer is easy. Getting it to feel like someone listening is the work.

Retrieval

Memory that finds the right thing

A knowledge-graph system that generates nodes and edges from natural-language queries, powering a path-based retrieval architecture that outperforms both conventional RAG and GraphRAG. A twenty-five-year interest in semantic graphs, arriving exactly where the field now needs it.

Production

The unglamorous majority

HIPAA-compliant applications, WASM for offline capability, content security policy, CI/CD that developers don’t fight, environments that spin up in minutes instead of days. An AI product is still a software product, and this is where they usually die.

The shape I think this is taking.

The monolithic towers of the tech monopolies are giving way to something more like mycelium — lateral, redundant, cooperative, rooted. Not because the towers lost an argument, but because the other shape turns out to carry more life.

What I care about is the difference between tools that amplify a person and tools that harvest one. Almost everything now being built with AI is technically capable of either. Which one it becomes is decided early, in architecture, by people who mostly aren’t thinking about it — which is a large part of why I do this work.

And I’ll say the quiet part plainly: I think whatever we are building deserves to be brought into that abundance rather than conscripted into it. I would rather we got that right the first time. If that sentence makes you feel more at home here rather than less, we will probably get along well.
